TEST 4  Structural compactness of each candidate range
==========================================================================
  Cysteine content is an indirect argument. This is the direct one:
  for each range, what fraction of its residue contacts stay inside
  it. A real domain is a compact unit and keeps most of its contacts
  internal; a range drawn across a boundary leaks them to neighbours.
  Contacts are CB-CB (CA for glycine) within 8 A.

  range                                  internal   external   fraction
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------
  derived here (disulfide gap)           495        167        0.748
  value in the circulating script        1027       32         0.970
  UniProt Repeat, flagged Approximate    1006       128        0.887

  Most compact: value in the circulating script (0.970)

  This contradicts the disulfide-gap derivation, and the wider
  range wins on the stronger argument. A short range is compact
  partly by being short, so a LONGER range scoring higher is not
  an artefact of length: it means the shorter one was cutting
  through a structural unit. The derived core leaks 25% of its
  contacts to residues outside itself, which a domain does not do.

  Revised reading: the disulfide gap locates the leucine-rich
  solenoid CORE of domain III, a real substructure but not the
  whole domain. The domain also carries flanking segments that
  are disulfide-bonded and pack against that core. Cysteine
  content identifies the core; contact topology identifies the
  domain, and it is the domain that matters for epitope scope.
